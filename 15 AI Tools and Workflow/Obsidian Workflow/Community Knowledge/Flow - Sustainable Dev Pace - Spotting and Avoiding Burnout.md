# Sustainable Dev Pace - Spotting and Avoiding Burnout

Roblox games are often live services: updates every week or two, events, player messages, Discord moderation, analytics. Many developers are teenagers or doing this beside school or a job. That mix is a recipe for burnout, and the DevForum has many threads from developers who lost all motivation mid-project. A game that needs you for two years needs a pace you can keep for two years. This note is practical, not medical; if you are struggling badly, talk to someone you trust or a health professional.

## What burnout is (and is not)

The WHO describes burn-out in ICD-11 as an occupational phenomenon, not a medical condition, resulting from chronic workplace stress that has not been managed, with three signs:

1. **Exhaustion**: energy depleted, even small tasks feel heavy.
2. **Mental distance or cynicism**: "the game is pointless", dreading opening Studio.
3. **Reduced effectiveness**: working longer but shipping less.

A normal tired week is not burnout. Weeks of all three together are a warning.

The wider games industry knows this pattern well: the IGDA's Developer Satisfaction Surveys have tracked crunch for years and repeatedly found long hours common. Solo devs crunch too; there is just nobody to notice.

## Early warning signs for game devs

- You stopped playtesting your own game because you do not enjoy it.
- Every bug report feels personal.
- You check analytics many times a day and your mood follows the graph.
- You keep starting new projects instead of finishing the current one.
- Devlog entries get shorter, then stop ([[Flow - Daily Devlog and Weekly Review in Obsidian]] makes this visible).

## Habits that keep a pace sustainable

- **Fixed working hours and at least one full day off** a week from the project, including Discord.
- **Plan at 70% of available time** ([[Flow - Weekly Planning and Timeboxing for Solo Roblox Devs]]).
- **Update cadence you can sustain.** A reliable update every two weeks beats weekly updates for two months then silence. Tell players the cadence.
- **Batch community time.** Answer Discord and DevForum messages in one block per day, not all day. Give trusted moderators power to act without you ([[Marketing - Community Manager Basics - Mod Team, Tone Guide and Escalation]]).
- **Analytics once a week**, on review day, not hourly.
- **Separate identity from metrics.** A flop game is data about the game, not about you.
- **Finish small things.** Shipping a small update gives more energy than another week on a giant feature.
- **Move your body, sleep, eat.** Unglamorous and the most effective item on this list.

## When you are already burnt out

1. **Reduce, do not quit abruptly.** Announce a short break or a slower cadence to players; most communities respect it.
2. **Put the game in maintenance mode**: only critical bug fixes; feature flags off for anything unstable.
3. **Take real time off**, then come back with one small, fun task.
4. **Talk to someone.** Friends, family, other devs; and a professional if low mood lasts.
5. **Consider help**: a co-developer, a moderator, or commissioning art or music, with clear agreements ([[Flow - Team and Freelancer IP on Roblox - Assignment, Group Ownership and Revenue Splits]]).

## Live-ops specific traps

- Event calendars that need you online at odd hours: schedule events with server time and pre-test them so they run without you.
- Players who demand updates aggressively: you set the roadmap; harassment goes to moderation, not to your to-do list ([[Marketing - Negative Feedback, Review Bombs and Leaks]]).
- AI tools making you feel you should ship faster: speed of generation is not the bottleneck; review, testing and your energy are.
