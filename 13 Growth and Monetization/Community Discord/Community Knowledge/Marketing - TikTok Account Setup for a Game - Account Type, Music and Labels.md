# Marketing - TikTok Account Setup for a Game - Account Type, Music and Labels

A creator who already has a personal TikTok has to decide: post the game there, or open a separate account for it? The answer affects **which music you can legally use**, which analytics you get and how labels work. This note covers the setup decisions. Where only third-party sources were available, the note says so. TikTok changes these menus often, so check in the app before you rely on any exact label.

## 1. Personal-creator account or game (business) account?

| | Your existing creator account | Separate game account set to **Business** |
|---|---|---|
| Reach on day 1 | your followers see it now | starts at zero |
| Music | general library (trending songs) is licensed for **personal, non-commercial** use | sound picker limited to the **Commercial Music Library (CML)**, which is cleared for commercial posts |
| Fit | "I'm making a game" creator content | official trailers, update teasers, ads |
| Ads/Spark | can boost posts | full Ads Manager tooling, website link, scheduler |

Third-party guides (Soundstripe, Social Media Today's report on TikTok's 2020 rule change) say business accounts are steered to the CML because popular tracks are licensed for personal entertainment only. A post that **promotes your own product** is commercial content, even from a personal account. Common practice for creator-devs:
- **Devlog and "I made a game" content** goes on your personal creator account, using original audio, your voice, game SFX or royalty-free music you have rights to.
- **Official trailers, teasers and anything you'll turn into an ad** use your own game music or CML tracks.
- **Safest default for both:** use **your own game soundtrack** and voiceover. It's licensed, it's recognisable and it builds an audio identity.

Don't use songs from Roblox's audio library in off-platform videos unless Roblox's own help article on using licensed music in videos allows it. In-game licenses don't automatically cover off-platform video. See [[Policy - Music Licensing - APM Library, Uploads and the 250 Track Rule]].

## 2. Profile that converts
- **Name/handle:** game name plus "game" or "roblox" (for example `@deadendsgame`) so search finds it.
- **Bio:** one line of premise and one call to action: "Zombie mall survival on Roblox. Play ↓".
- **Link:** a **Share Link** (`tt_bio_yyyymm`). Note that link-in-bio availability can depend on account type and follower count **(unverified: thresholds change)**.
- **Pinned videos (up to 3):** best trailer, best gameplay moment, latest update.

## 3. Labels and disclosure
- TikTok has a **content disclosure** setting for promotional posts. Third-party guides describe a "Promotional content" or "Branded content" label for your own brand, and a "Paid partnership" label when promoting a third party **(exact wording unverified)**.
- The FTC says a platform's built-in label **isn't automatically enough**. Responsibility "rests with the influencer and the brand". When you pay creators, also require an on-screen and spoken disclosure. See [[Marketing - Paid Promo Disclosure - FTC, ASA and Platform Labels]].
- Promoting **your own** game on your own channel is obviously your product, but a cross-post on a personal account that most followers don't know is yours benefits from "my game" in the caption.

## 4. Age settings matter for a Roblox audience
TikTok's minimum age is 13. Its 2021 safety update made under-16 accounts **private by default** and age-restricted direct messages and LIVE. Many young Roblox players therefore can't DM you or join some features. Never ask kids to DM you, never collect their contact details, and keep comments moderated (filter keywords on).

## Setup checklist
- [ ] Decide: personal channel, game account, or both (link them in bios)
- [ ] Game account on Business if it will post ads or official trailers
- [ ] Original or CML audio for commercial posts; no Roblox library tracks off-platform
- [ ] Share Link in bio; 3 pinned videos
- [ ] Comment keyword filters on (scam terms like "free robux")
