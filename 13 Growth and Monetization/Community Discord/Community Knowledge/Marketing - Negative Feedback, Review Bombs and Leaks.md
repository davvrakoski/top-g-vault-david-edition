# Marketing - Negative Feedback, Review Bombs and Leaks

Every game that gets noticed also gets hate comments, a wave of dislikes after an unpopular change, and sooner or later a leak: a playtester posts unreleased content, someone datamines the client files, or a staff member shares a screenshot early. As a creator you're also personally visible, so the criticism lands on your channel, not only the game. A plan made in advance turns these into manageable events. This note covers sorting feedback, responding to a dislike or review wave, and handling leaks.

## 1. Sort negative feedback before you react
| Type | Signal | Response |
|---|---|---|
| **Bug / broken thing** | specific, repeated | thank them, log it, give a fix ETA range |
| **Design disagreement** | "the nerf ruined X" | explain the reason once, watch the data, change course if retention agrees |
| **Expectation gap** | "the video showed Y" | check your marketing honesty; fix the message or the feature |
| **Pure hostility** | insults, no content | don't argue; moderate per rules |
| **Coordinated wave** | sudden spike from new accounts, same phrases | lock threads, one pinned statement, wait 24-48 h |

Research reported by Bond University found that well-managed feedback can improve games, and that **censoring** reviews makes it harder to learn from players. Remove abuse, not criticism.

## 2. The 24-hour response template
For a wave of dislikes or angry posts after a change:
1. **Within hours:** one pinned message on Discord and the community wall: "We hear you about X. Here's why we changed it. We're watching the numbers this week and will post an update on <day>."
2. **Data check** (2-3 days): D1 retention, session length, purchase refunds or complaints, like ratio.
3. **Decision post:** revert, adjust, or keep **with numbers**. Players accept "we kept it because..." more readily than silence.
4. **Never** insult players or mock complaints in your creator videos. The Xsolla guide gives Ooblets as a case where a dismissive tone turned a small issue into a big backlash.

**Don't fake the score:** paying or asking alts and friends to like the game or post praise is deceptive. The FTC treats fake engagement as deceptive, and Roblox's Standards ban misleading tactics to boost discovery.

## 3. Your creator channel's comment section
- Turn on keyword filters (scams, slurs, "free robux").
- Pin a helpful comment (patch-note link, known issues).
- Make a **response video** only for real, repeated issues, and keep it calm and short. Don't make drama content about your own players.

## 4. Leaks: prevention
- **Playtest builds** in a separate private place with **only** the content under test. Never ship unreleased models in the live place where they can be extracted.
- Assets loaded on the client can be dumped by exploit tools. Keep secret content **server-side** (ServerStorage) until release.
- Playtesters: a written "please don't post" agreement (even an informal one sets expectations), and a **watermark** (tester name, small and in a corner) on early builds.
- Staff screenshots: one shared "safe to post" folder. Everything else is internal.

## 5. Leaks: response
1. **Don't panic-confirm.** "That's work in progress and may change" is enough.
2. If it's **your copyrighted content reposted** (stolen place, ripped models), use the takedown routes in [[Policy - Copyright and Trademark Takedowns - Notices and Counter Notices]].
3. Remove the leaker's tester access quietly. Public shaming makes it a story.
4. Turn it into marketing when the content is good: "Well, you've seen it. It's coming Saturday."
5. Fix the hole (which build or channel leaked it).

## Pitfalls
- Deleting every critical message: people notice and trust drops.
- Promising a revert under pressure before you've looked at data.
- Leaking the roadmap yourself in a livestream. Agree with yourself what's on and off the record.
