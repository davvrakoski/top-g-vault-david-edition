# Marketing - Paid Promo Disclosure - FTC, ASA and Platform Labels

If you pay a YouTuber in Robux, give them free in-game items or an exclusive code, or post about your own game from an account people don't know is yours, that's an **endorsement with a material connection**. US (FTC) and UK (ASA/CMA) rules require it to be disclosed clearly. The FTC also puts responsibility on **the advertiser**, meaning you, the developer, to brief and monitor creators. Not legal advice; this is a practical summary of the official guidance.

## 1. When disclosure is needed (FTC Endorsement Guides FAQ, revised 2023)
Disclose any connection that a significant share of viewers wouldn't expect and that could affect how they judge the endorsement:
- **Payment** of any kind: cash, Robux, revenue share.
- **Free stuff** given in hope of coverage: early access, admin perks, exclusive items, gamepasses.
- **Affiliate or commission links** and personal codes that earn the creator something.
- **Family, friends and employees or team members** promoting your game. Listing the employer in a bio isn't enough.
- An ongoing relationship: each new post needs disclosure too.

## 2. What counts as clear
- No required wording. The FAQ's examples are along the lines of "This is an ad for [game]" or "[Studio] paid me to make this".
- "#ad", "Ad:" or "ad" at the start are treated as roughly equal and acceptable. "Sponsored by X" is clearer than a bare "#sponsored".
- **Ambiguous or weak:** "#partner", "#ambassador", "#collab"-style tags, "Gifted" without naming who gave it, and "thanks to X".
- **Placement:** in the video itself and near the endorsement. **Description-only, comments-only or a "disclosure" link is not enough.**
- Match the medium: visual endorsements need visual disclosure, spoken ones need spoken disclosure, and videos usually need **both**.
- **Livestreams:** repeat the disclosure periodically.
- **Short videos:** large superimposed on-screen text. Small description text isn't sufficient.
- **Platform labels** (YouTube's branded-content checkbox, TikTok's disclosure toggle) **are not automatically sufficient**. Use them *and* add your own disclosure.

## 3. UK: ASA / CAP influencer guide
The ASA's influencer guide (4th edition, 2026, written with the CMA) covers when content is an ad, affiliate disclosure and labelling, and adds fake-review rules under the **DMCC Act**. Long-standing ASA practice is a clear, upfront **"Ad"** label. Check the current PDF for exact label rules before a UK campaign **(label details not re-verified from the 2026 PDF)**.

## 4. Platform tools
- **YouTube:** in the upload flow, under *Paid promotion*, tick **"Yes, my video includes branded content"**. YouTube shows a disclosure label at the start of the video. YouTube's page also says creators and brands remain responsible for their legal obligations.
- **TikTok:** turn on the content disclosure setting for paid partnerships. See [[Marketing - TikTok Account Setup for a Game - Account Type, Music and Labels]].
- **Roblox affiliate links (historic):** the Creator Affiliate pilot (replaced by Creator Rewards in July 2025) required an FTC disclosure on affiliate links. The principle still applies to Share Links that earn you audience-expansion revenue when used by **other people** you pay.

## 5. Your duties as the advertiser
The FTC expects a "reasonable program": brief creators in writing, require disclosure, **review posts before they go live** where possible, check them afterwards, and act on problems. A brief template:
```
Disclosure: say "This video is sponsored by <game>" in the first 30 s
AND show on-screen text "AD - sponsored by <game>" for 3+ s.
Tick the platform's paid/branded content setting.
Don't claim features we don't have. Don't say "not sponsored".
Send the final cut 48 h before posting.
```

## 6. Reviews and fake engagement
- Never pay for positive comments, likes or reviews, and never make a reward depend on a positive review.
- Don't ask your team to post "fan" comments without disclosure.
- The FTC's guide warns that disclosures may **not work for young children**, which makes this stricter for Roblox audiences. See [[Marketing - Marketing to Kids and Teens - CARU, Made for Kids and Age Rules]].
