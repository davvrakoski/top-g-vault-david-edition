# Marketing - Short-Form Formats for Roblox Games - Devlog, I Made a Game, Trends and Teasers

A content plan built on repeatable **formats** beats waiting for one viral idea. Each format below has a known shape, so you can film several in one session and post on a schedule. They aim at different audiences, so mix them on purpose: **player-facing** formats bring players, and **dev-facing** formats bring followers and collaborators.

## Format menu

| Format | Audience | Shape (15-45 s) | Best when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **"I made a Roblox game where..."** | players + curious devs | hook premise, then 3 quick features, then "should I add X?" | pre-launch, premise is high-concept |
| **Build progress / before-after** | devs + fans | greybox, then hard cut to final, then gameplay | big visual upgrades |
| **Devlog bite** | devs | problem, then attempt, then fix, then result | weekly; feeds the long-form devlog |
| **Gameplay moment** | players | real clip of something funny or clutch, no talking | game is live |
| **Trend edit** | players | game footage cut to a trending sound or meme format | the trend fits your genre |
| **Update teaser** | existing players | 3 shots of new content, date on screen, "Saturday 5pm" | 3-7 days before an update |
| **Secret / easter egg reveal** | players | "you missed this" plus location | after launch, cheap to make |
| **Comment reply** | engaged followers | reply-to-comment video that builds the requested thing | when comments ask for features |
| **Player reaction / co-op chaos** | players | friends playing, voice on | social or party games |

## How to choose the mix
- **Pre-launch (no game to play yet):** "I made a game", build progress and comment replies. They build an audience that wants the launch. Ask questions in the video ("which map first?") so comments become your next video.
- **Launch week:** gameplay moments, trend edits and the teaser of the first update. Every post carries the channel's Share Link in bio and pinned comment.
- **Live game:** a mostly gameplay-first mix, plus one devlog bite a week to keep the "creator" identity.

A DevForum case (LOFTS, 2020) went from about 2 daily players to thousands after **other players** posted "aesthetic Roblox" TikToks. Make the game easy for players to film, with a freecam-like spectate mode, readable UI and photo spots, and repost their clips with credit.

## Production habits that scale
1. **Batch film:** one recording session gives you 5-10 clips. Keep a running "clip-worthy" folder.
2. **One template per format:** the same font, text position and end card, so posting takes 10 minutes.
3. **Series titles:** "Day 12 of making a zombie game" gives followers a reason to come back.
4. **Test on cheap platforms first.** Chris Zukowski (How To Market A Game) suggests testing a GIF and headline combination on easy, low-stakes posts, then reusing the winner where effort is higher.
5. **Repost winners:** a clip that did well on TikTok goes to Shorts and X a few days later, with the native caption style of each.

## Pitfalls
- Posting only devlogs and wondering why there are no players. Devlogs mostly reach developers.
- Chasing a trend that has nothing to do with your game. Watch time drops and the algorithm learns the wrong audience.
- Showing features that won't ship. The comments will remember.
