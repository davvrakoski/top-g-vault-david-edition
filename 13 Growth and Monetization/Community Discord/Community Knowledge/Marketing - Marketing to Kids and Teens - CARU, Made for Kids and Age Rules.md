# Marketing - Marketing to Kids and Teens - CARU, Made for Kids and Age Rules

A large share of Roblox players are children, so your off-platform marketing reaches them too. That brings stricter rules than adult-audience game marketing: children's advertising guidelines in the US, COPPA on YouTube, age limits on social apps, and Roblox's own age gates on links. This note collects what applies to a creator-dev promoting a Roblox game. Not legal advice.

## 1. CARU (US children's advertising self-regulation)
- CARU (BBB National Programs) revised its guidelines effective **1 Jan 2022**. They now cover children **under 13** on any platform, and include a section on **in-app and in-game advertising and purchases**.
- The core principle: advertising to kids must be **clearly identifiable as advertising**.
- **CARU's 2023 Roblox decision** (reported June 2023) found that ads on Roblox often lacked disclosures children could understand. Branded experiences and integrated video content lacked labels, and many **Video Stars** didn't disclose their Roblox connection in kid-friendly language. CARU said the word **"commission"** was unclear to children.
- **What that means for your creator deals:** disclosures in **simple words** ("Ad", "[Studio] paid me to play this"), **both spoken and on screen**, and repeated in longer videos. The FTC's FAQ adds that disclosures may not work for younger children at all, so be "particularly careful" with child-directed promos.

## 2. YouTube "made for kids"
- Under COPPA every channel must set the audience. A video is *made for kids* if children are the primary audience **or** one of the target audiences ("mixed audience").
- Factors YouTube lists: subject matter, intended or actual audience, child actors, **characters, toys or animated content that appeal to children**, child-directed language, simple games, songs and stories, and whether it's advertised to kids. No single factor decides it. YouTube lists "a gaming video with adult humour" and "animated content that appeals to everyone" as general-audience examples.
- **Consequences:** wrong settings risk YouTube penalties and legal consequences under COPPA. The FTC can take a different view than YouTube's systems.
- **Feature impact you'll notice:** end screens aren't available on made-for-kids videos (YouTube help). Made-for-kids content is widely reported to lose comments and personalised ads too **(not confirmed on the help page fetched)**.
- Typical devlogs (talking about game development, Studio on screen) are generally aimed at a teen and adult audience. A cartoony "obby for little kids" trailer may be made for kids. Decide per video, honestly.

## 3. Social-app age rules (brief)
- TikTok: minimum age 13. Its 2021 teen-safety update made **under-16 accounts private by default** and age-restricted DMs and LIVE. Many players can't message you, which is a reason not to run "DM me for a code" campaigns.
- Roblox Social Links: only **age-verified 16+** viewers see them. Under-16 players won't see your Discord or YouTube link on the game page.
- Discord's terms require users to be 13+ (minimum age varies by country) **(not re-verified here)**.

## 4. Data and contact
- Don't collect emails, phone numbers or real names from players for giveaways or newsletters unless you have a COPPA-compliant process. For under-13s that means verifiable parental consent.
- Giveaways: Roblox bans Robux or real-money contests run through Roblox content unless Roblox is involved. Off-platform giveaways aimed at kids add sweepstakes-law complexity. Prefer free in-game codes for everyone.
- Never ask players to send account details, passwords or "proof" screenshots that show personal info.

## 5. Tone rules for kid-heavy audiences
- No fake urgency ("only 5 minutes left!" when it isn't true). Roblox's notification rules ban false time pressure in-game too.
- No "free Robux" bait, even as a joke thumbnail. It's the most common scam on the platform.
- Separate your **creator persona's** adult humour from the game's official channels.

## Checklist
- [ ] Every paid creator: kid-friendly spoken + on-screen "Ad" disclosure
- [ ] YouTube audience set per video, honestly
- [ ] No contact-data collection from players
- [ ] No contests with Robux prizes; codes for everyone instead
- [ ] No fake urgency or "free Robux" style hooks
