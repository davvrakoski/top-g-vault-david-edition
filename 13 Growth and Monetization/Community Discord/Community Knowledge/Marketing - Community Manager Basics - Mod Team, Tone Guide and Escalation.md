# Marketing - Community Manager Basics - Mod Team, Tone Guide and Escalation

Once your Discord and Roblox community pass a few hundred active members, the developer can't also be the moderator, the support desk and the hype person. Community management is a separate job: say what's happening, collect feedback, enforce rules, and protect the dev's time. This note covers how a solo or small team splits that work, recruits volunteer moderators safely, and writes a tone guide so every staff message sounds like the same studio.

## 1. Roles (even if one person holds several)

| Role | Owns | Doesn't do |
|---|---|---|
| **Community manager (CM)** | announcements, patch-note posts, events, feedback summaries for the dev, crisis messages | ban decisions alone on edge cases |
| **Moderators** | rule enforcement, scam and raid cleanup, ticket triage | promise features or dates |
| **Support helpers** (trusted members) | answer FAQ in #help, point to bug-report form | access to admin tools |
| **Developer** | roadmap, fixes, final say on policy | day-to-day moderation |

## 2. Recruiting volunteer mods
Discord's community pages put **trust** first, then personality, mindset, game knowledge and **availability** across your players' time zones. They also say to first check whether you need more mods, and to watch for **burnout**.
- Recruit from **calm, consistent helpers**, not only the loudest or most active member.
- Short application (age 16+ is common practice, time zone, hours per week, how they'd handle 2 sample situations).
- **Trial period** (2-4 weeks) with limited permissions: timeout, not ban.
- Require **2FA** on every staff account. Discord recommends it, and Discord's 2FA-for-moderation server setting enforces it.
- **Announce new mods** publicly so members know who they are (also a Discord recommendation).
- On Roblox, give mods community roles with **only** the permissions they need (for example forum post remover, not experience editing).

Never give game **edit permission** to community staff. Edit access also lets them use freecam and debug UI in live servers.

## 3. Tone guide (one page, pinned in #staff)
- **Voice:** friendly, direct, a bit playful. Never sarcastic toward players.
- **Never promise dates** you don't control. Say "we're aiming for" or "soon™". Only the dev posts dates.
- **Acknowledge, then inform:** "That bug is annoying, sorry. It's on our list, and here's the workaround for now."
- **No public arguments.** Move to DMs or tickets after one reply.
- **Kid-safe always:** no swearing, no adult jokes, no asking members for personal info.
- **Same words for the same thing:** one name for each currency, mode and item, matching the in-game names.

## 4. Escalation ladder
1. **Mod handles:** spam, scams, rude members (timeout, then warn, then ban per rules).
2. **CM handles:** repeated complaints on one topic, creator or partner contact, heated threads (post a pinned answer).
3. **Dev is pinged:** exploits, data-loss reports, payment issues, safety reports involving minors (also report through Roblox's and Discord's official tools), credible threats.
4. **Off-platform authority:** credible threats of violence go to platform safety teams and, where appropriate, law enforcement. The Xsolla guide cites a studio taking legal action over a streamer's threats.

## 5. Weekly CM rhythm (2-3 h)
- Mon: feedback digest for the dev (top 5 requests, top 5 bugs, mood in one line).
- Wed: event or sneak peek post.
- Fri: patch-note or progress post; clip of the week.
- Daily 15 min: skim #help and #bugs; tag duplicates.

## 6. Prevent burnout
Rotate "on duty" days, give mods a private lounge, thank them publicly, and accept that volunteers leave. Write the rules down so a new mod can start in a day.

## Pitfalls
- Mods posting their own opinion on unreleased features as if it were official.
- Overreacting to a small, loud group. The Xsolla guide warns that reactive moves can make a toxic moment worse.
- Letting the dev answer every message, so nothing ships.
