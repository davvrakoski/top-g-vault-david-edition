# Marketing - X and Bluesky for Roblox Devs - What to Post and How to Grow

Text-and-image social platforms rarely bring many players directly. Their value is elsewhere: the **Roblox developer scene** (collaborators, Roblox staff, other devs), **content creators** who watch for new games, and a public record of progress that press and partners can check. Roblox's Acquisition guide says to post updates, teasers and screenshots regularly and to **reply to larger accounts** to grow. This note covers what works on X and Bluesky, and what to skip.

## 1. What each platform is for
| | X (Twitter) | Bluesky |
|---|---|---|
| Roblox dev community | the established home of most Roblox devs and studios | growing; many artists and indie devs moved there |
| Discovery | algorithmic "For you" plus replies | follows, custom **feeds** and **starter packs** |
| Best use | GIF progress posts, code drops, creator discovery | the same posts, plus being added to dev starter packs and feeds |
| Link visibility | link posts are widely reported to reach fewer people, so put links in a reply (practice reported by Chris Zukowski) | links fine |

Cross-post the same content to both. It costs one extra minute.

## 2. Posts that perform
1. **Announcement post.** Zukowski's research found an announcement is usually a dev's **best-performing** tweet. Write "Announcing <game>", one-line description, GIF or trailer, clear call to action, and ask friends to repost. Don't bury the news inside the GIF.
2. **Short looping GIF or MP4 (3-8 s)** of one satisfying thing, with a single-line caption that names the feeling ("the zombies pile up on the escalator").
3. **Before/after** builds.
4. **"How I made X" threads** with 3-5 images: for the dev audience, they get reposted a lot.
5. **Questions to followers** ("which zombie should be the boss?") that feed future content.

## 3. Test cheaply, then spend effort
Zukowski's "beta test your marketing" advice: post GIF and headline combinations where posting is cheap (X, TikTok). When one clearly beats the rest, **reuse that exact pairing** on harder channels: trailer opening, Roblox thumbnail text, ad creative. His example also shows engagement can fool you: a tweet with high likes produced few store actions. Measure **Share Link plays**, not likes.

## 4. Bluesky specifics
- **Custom domain handle:** set your handle to your studio domain (e.g. `@deadendsgame.com`) by adding a DNS TXT record (`_atproto`) with your account DID. Bluesky's blog describes this as a self-verification method.
- **Starter packs:** shareable bundles of accounts and up to **3 custom feeds**. New users can follow everything in one tap. Launch coverage (TechCrunch, June 2024) said 50 accounts per pack. Later third-party guides say 150 **(current limit unverified)**. A Lancaster University study found starter packs drove up to 43% of follows at peak times, and accounts included in them gained substantially more followers. **Get into Roblox-dev and game-dev starter packs**, and make one for your team.
- Join or create a **custom feed** for Roblox dev content and post with the keywords it filters on.

## 5. Rhythm (solo creator)
- 3-5 posts a week: 2 visual progress posts, 1 dev or tech post, 1 community reply or repost.
- Spend 10 minutes a day **replying** usefully to bigger Roblox dev and creator accounts. That's the growth method Roblox's guide names.
- Keep the **Share Link** in your profile link (X) and bio (Bluesky).
- Tags: Roblox devs commonly use **#RobloxDev**. Use one or two relevant tags at most.

## Pitfalls
- Posting only links ("play my game ↓"). Nobody shares an ad.
- Arguments with other devs or players in public. Screenshots last forever.
- Expecting X to fill your servers. In the indie post-mortems Zukowski reviewed, Twitter didn't drive much compared with streamers and festivals. Treat it as networking.
