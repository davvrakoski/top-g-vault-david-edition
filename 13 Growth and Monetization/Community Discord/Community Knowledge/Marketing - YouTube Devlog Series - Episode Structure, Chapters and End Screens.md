# Marketing - YouTube Devlog Series - Episode Structure, Chapters and End Screens

A devlog series turns months of solo work into a story people follow and want to see finished. It builds a loyal audience, a stack of evergreen search content and a launch-day crowd. A pile of unrelated videos doesn't, so treat the series as one show with recurring parts. This note gives an episode template, YouTube's exact rules for chapters and end screens, and how to keep the series pointed at players, not only other developers.

## 1. Series spine
- **Premise in the series title:** "Making a Roblox zombie mall game" beats "Devlog #7". Episodes: "I added a boss that hunts you (Devlog 7)".
- **Season arcs:** Season 1 runs from prototype to playable test, Season 2 to launch, Season 3 covers live updates. Each season ends on a milestone that viewers can **play**.
- **Cadence:** every 2-3 weeks is realistic solo. Consistency matters more than frequency. Announce the next episode's topic at the end.
- **Playlist** per season, in order, so binge-watchers go from episode 1 forward.

## 2. Episode template (8-15 min)

| Time | Beat | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00-0:20 | **Cold open**: the best result of this episode, playing | hook (gameplay first, as trailer advice also says) |
| 0:20-0:45 | the goal of this episode, stated in one sentence | sets stakes |
| main | 2-4 **problem, then attempt, then failure/twist, then fix** segments | the story |
| late | **playtest** footage: friends or the community reacting | proof it's fun |
| end | what's next + "play the test build / join the Discord, links below" | conversion |

Show the game running for most of the runtime. Talking-head and code-on-screen segments should be short and end on a visible result.

## 3. Chapters (YouTube rules)
Add timestamps to the description:
- The first one **must be 00:00**.
- At least **three** timestamps, in ascending order.
- Each chapter **at least 10 seconds**.
- Manual chapters override automatic ones.

Name chapters after outcomes ("Boss AI finally works") so they work as search snippets too.

## 4. End screens (YouTube rules)
- Can cover the **last 5-20 seconds**. The video must be **at least 25 s** long.
- Up to **4 elements**: video (latest, best-for-viewer or a specific one), playlist, subscribe, channel, and **link** (link requires YouTube Partner Program).
- **Not available on videos set as made for kids.**

Standard layout: **next episode** (or the season playlist), plus **subscribe**, plus the "how to play" guide video. Leave the last 15 s of the edit visually calm so elements don't cover the action.

## 5. Description template
```
Episode 7 of making DEAD ENDS, a zombie mall survival game on Roblox.
▶ Play the test: <share link yt_devlog_ep07>
💬 Community: see Social Links on the game page / <discord invite>
00:00 The boss that hunts you
00:42 Why the first AI broke
...
Music: original soundtrack
```
Use a different Share Link for each episode, so you can see which episodes actually bring players.

## Pitfalls
- Episodes about engine internals with no visible payoff. Developers like them, players skip them.
- Long gaps with no explanation. Post a 1-minute Short or community post ("ep 8 is late because...").
- Promising features on camera that never ship.
