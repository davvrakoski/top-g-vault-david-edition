# Marketing - Content Calendar for a Solo Dev Creator

When you build the game *and* make the videos, marketing either eats your dev time or gets skipped for weeks. A calendar fixes both. Recording happens **inside** dev sessions, editing gets fixed slots, and every game update already has its posts planned. Roblox's Acquisition guide says to "post regularly" with updates, teasers and screenshots. This note gives a weekly template, a per-update template and a simple tracking sheet.

## 1. Content pillars (keep 3-4)
| Pillar | Example | Share of posts |
|---|---|---|
| **Gameplay moments** | funny or clutch clips, boss kills | ~40% |
| **Building the game** | before/after, "I made...", devlog bites | ~30% |
| **Community** | clip of the week, fan art, polls, comment replies | ~20% |
| **Announcements** | update teaser, event, codes | ~10% (more in launch weeks) |

## 2. Weekly template (about 5 h of marketing work)
| Day | Make | Post |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | 30 min: tag clips from last week's recordings (Replay Buffer / chapter markers) | TikTok + Shorts: gameplay moment |
| Tue | 1 h: edit 2 shorts from template | X / Bluesky: GIF progress |
| Wed | (dev day; recording runs in the background) | TikTok + Shorts: "building the game" |
| Thu | 1 h: devlog script or edit block | Discord: sneak peek |
| Fri | 30 min: community digest | TikTok + Shorts: community / poll; Discord playtest night |
| Sat | update day (if scheduled) | Roblox announcement (60 chars), Discord notes, teaser-to-live post |
| Sun | 30 min: review numbers | — |

Every other week, swap one short slot for the long-form devlog episode.

## 3. Per-update template (count back from release day D)
| When | Action |
|---|---|
| D-14 | create the Roblox **event** (private); nominate for **Today's Picks** if notable |
| D-10 | record update footage in a capture place; brief partner creators |
| D-7 | event **public**; submit for Off-Platform Featuring (needs ≥ 7 days' notice) |
| D-3 | teaser short: 3 shots plus date |
| D-1 | countdown post; creator early access |
| D | release; announcement; notes; live stream or playtest |
| D+2 | "what you missed" clip; respond to feedback |
| D+7 | review: share-link plays, D7 of new players, update visit rate |

## 4. Tracking sheet (one row per post)
`date, platform, format, hook text, length, views, avg % watched, share-link plays (from Acquisition), notes`

Check every Sunday:
- Which **hook** held viewers (avg % watched, or viewed vs swiped on Shorts).
- Which **format** brought **plays**, not just views (share links per platform).
- Make more of the top 20% and drop the bottom 20%.

TikTok's own guidance is to refresh creatives when performance clearly declines. For organic posting, that means retiring a format when it stops holding viewers, even if you like it.

## 5. Batch and buffer
- Keep **5 finished shorts in the buffer** for weeks when dev work takes over.
- Film several hooks for each clip (see the hooks note) and schedule the variants on different days.
- Recycle: a post that did well can run again 6-8 weeks later on another platform, or with a new hook.

## Pitfalls
- Planning more than you can keep up. Three posts a week, every week, beats ten in launch week and then nothing.
- Measuring by likes. Measure by **plays and retention** from share links.
- Content about features months away. Keep teasers to what ships within about two weeks.
