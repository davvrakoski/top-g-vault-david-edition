# Marketing - Making a Roblox Game Trailer - Gameplay First Structure

A trailer has one job: within seconds, show what you **do** in the game and why it's fun. The most common indie mistake, analysed by GameDiscoverCo (Simon Carless, 2022) with input from trailer editor Derek Lieu, is the movie-style slow reveal: logos, a moody pan, story text, and gameplay 15-30 seconds in. Viewers on a store page or a feed skip straight to raw gameplay. A Roblox trailer is cut in at least three versions (YouTube, vertical social, Roblox game page) and has to work in all of them.

## 1. Structure: "tell, show, repeat"
Lieu's reported method: introduce one idea, then **immediately show gameplay that proves it**, then the next idea, and build up the way a good tutorial does.

| Time (60-90 s cut) | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0-3 s | **Gameplay first**: the core verb in action (no logo) |
| 3-10 s | premise in 3-6 words of text, over more gameplay |
| 10-40 s | 3-4 "tell, show" pairs: feature, proof clip, each one escalating |
| 40-55 s | social proof or the "wow" moment: big fight, co-op chaos, boss |
| 55-65 s | **title card + "Play free on Roblox"** + date or "out now" |
| last 2-3 s | a short gameplay "button" (a joke or sting) after the title |

Lieu often **starts from the music**: pick the track first, then cut to its structure (intro, build, drop, outro). Hits on the beat are what make cheap footage feel professional.

## 2. Three deliverables from one edit
| Version | Specs | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **YouTube trailer** | 16:9, 60-90 s, 1080p+ | Share Link in description; end screen to devlog playlist |
| **Vertical social cut** | 9:16, 15-30 s | hook in the first 1-2 s; TikTok wants the proposition within 3 s |
| **Roblox game-page video** | uploaded via Dashboard > Configurations > Places > **Videos** | strict rules, see below |

**Roblox page video rules** (Creator Docs, thumbnails): authentic gameplay, with freecam angles, highlight reels, minor colour adjustments and your logo allowed. **Not allowed:** gameplay or UI that isn't in the game, graphics enhanced beyond what players see, real-life footage, **voice-over, narration, voice chat or music with lyrics**, and ad-style text such as "50% off" or "Free UGC!". Rejected videos still use one of the **3 uploads per month**. Cut a separate page version **without voice-over and lyrics**.

## 3. Shot and footage rules
- Capture clean (freecam, no UI or nameplates). See [[Marketing - Clean Roblox Footage - Freecam, Hiding UI and Staging a Shot]].
- Every shot shows **motion and consequence**: something hits, breaks, scores or reacts.
- Vary shot scale: wide, medium, close.
- Keep shots short: 1-3 s in the build, longer for the hero moment.
- Use real players or friends for co-op. Empty servers read as a dead game.

## 4. Text and audio
- On-screen text: few words, big and readable on a phone. State verbs and stakes, not adjectives ("Hold the mall until dawn" beats "An epic experience").
- Music: your own soundtrack or properly licensed music. Mix SFX on top of the music (impacts, UI pings) so it feels like gameplay.
- Always end on a **clear call to action** and the game name.

## 5. How many trailers?
Indies can't afford the AAA habit of a dozen trailers. Make **one strong gameplay trailer**, then short update teasers for each big update. Re-cut the main trailer every 6-12 months as the game improves.

## Checklist
- [ ] Gameplay in the first frame; no logo intro
- [ ] 3-4 tell-show pairs; music-driven cut
- [ ] 16:9, 9:16 and Roblox-page versions exported
- [ ] Page version: no VO, no lyrics, no promo text
- [ ] Share Link in every description
