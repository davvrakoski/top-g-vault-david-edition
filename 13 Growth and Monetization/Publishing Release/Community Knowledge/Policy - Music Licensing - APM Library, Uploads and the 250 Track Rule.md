# Music Licensing - APM Library, Uploads and the 250 Track Rule

> Not legal advice. Summary of the Help Center "Using Licensed Music on Roblox", the "Music on Roblox" section of the Creator Terms, and the Audio Upload License Agreement (edited 2026-07-28), checked 2026-10-25.

Music is the asset type most likely to be pulled automatically: Roblox scans uploaded audio for IP infringement, and copyrighted songs were mass-removed starting in 2018. There are two legal paths for music in your game: Roblox's **Licensed Music** library, or audio you **fully own** (or properly license) and upload yourself. Anything else is a takedown waiting to happen. For upload limits and privacy settings see [[Roblox Audio Rules - Uploads, Privacy and Creator Store Sounds]]; this note covers the rights side.

## Path 1: Roblox Licensed Music

Roblox licensed a large catalog from **APM Music** (plus sound effects) that is royalty-free for use on Roblox. The Creator Terms set the conditions:

- **Roblox-only:** a personal, non-exclusive, revocable license to synchronise the tracks into your game, only on Roblox and only while Roblox offers them.
- **Revocable any time:** if Roblox loses or revokes a track, you must remove it immediately. Plan for tracks disappearing.
- **250 distinct tracks** maximum in one game at a time (boomboxes count toward it). You can swap tracks.
- **No music service:** you may not build a streaming service or music library, and you may not **charge players to listen to a specific track**. A paid "song request" or paid radio station built on Licensed Music breaks this.
- **Edits allowed in-game:** you may use portions, samples, and Studio sound properties to make derivative versions, inside the game only.
- **No export:** nothing that lets players download or extract the audio.

## Path 2: uploading your own music

The Audio Upload License Agreement and Creator Terms require that you **own or have paid for all needed rights** (sound recording, composition, public performance) and keep paying for them. You grant Roblox a perpetual worldwide license to stream, reproduce and display the audio and its artist and track names on the platform, and you indemnify Roblox against claims.

Extra rules for musicians:

- **PRO members** (performing rights organisations) must notify their PRO in writing of the royalty-free license they give Roblox. Co-writers must do the same, and you need written proof of their approval.
- If you assigned your song to a **publisher**, you need its written consent.
- If you are signed to a **record label**, your contract may forbid this upload; check it.
- Owning a recording but not the underlying composition is not enough.
- The audio must also meet the Community Standards (no profanity, drug or sexual references).

## Path 3: free and royalty-free libraries

Third-party "royalty-free" tracks are allowed only if their license permits use and redistribution inside a game on Roblox, and you can prove it. Keep the license file. See [[Free Music for Roblox Games - License Table]] for checked sources.

## What breaks games in practice

- Popular songs uploaded "because others have them": automated scans reject or remove them.
- A licensed track removed by Roblox, leaving silent menus; always have fallback audio.
- Paid "radio" passes using Licensed Music.
- Music in **videos you make off-platform** follows a separate help article ("Using Licensed Music in Videos"); in-game rights do not automatically cover your trailer.

## Checklist

- [ ] Every music asset is either Roblox Licensed Music or has a stored license/ownership proof
- [ ] Track count under 250 per game
- [ ] No feature charges Robux to hear a particular licensed song
- [ ] Fallback track configured if a licensed track fails to load
