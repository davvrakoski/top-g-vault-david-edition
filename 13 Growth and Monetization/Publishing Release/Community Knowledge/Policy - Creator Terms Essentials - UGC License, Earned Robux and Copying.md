# Creator Terms Essentials - UGC License, Earned Robux and Copying

> Not legal advice. Our own summary of the Creator Terms section of the Roblox Terms of Use, checked 2026-10-25 (page last edited 2026-10-07). A rewritten version takes effect 2026-11-01; re-check after that date.

The Creator Terms are the part of the Terms of Use that apply the moment you open Studio. They decide who owns what you build, what Roblox may do with it, and which Robux can ever become real money. Knowing them stops two classic mistakes: thinking a game you built is "yours" in every sense, and thinking every Robux in your balance can be cashed out.

## Ownership and the license you give Roblox

- **You keep your copyright** in what you create and publish (except Modified Classic Avatars, see below).
- **You grant Roblox a broad license**: perpetual, worldwide, non-exclusive, royalty-free and sublicensable, to host, transfer, translate and display your UGC, including for marketing Roblox. This survives even if your account is terminated, and the marketing part survives a GDPR right-to-be-forgotten request.
- **Items you sold stay with buyers.** If you delete a game item, Roblox may still let players keep using what they bought.
- **You must own or be authorised for every part** of what you publish, and you pay any third party you owe for it. You also agree to indemnify Roblox if your content causes a claim.

## Roblox-owned material you can use only on Roblox

- **Templates** and **Other Roblox Content**: licensed to you only on the Services, revocable.
- **Classic Avatars (R6, R15 and their derivatives)**: you may customise them, but Roblox owns modifications, and you may never use them off-platform (merch, film, books, physical goods).
- **Builder font**: only under its own license.
- **The name "Roblox" or "Blox"**: banned in game titles, usernames and community names (games published before 2022-06-22 are grandfathered).

## Earned Robux versus other Robux

Only Robux earned in full compliance with the Terms and Community Standards count as Earned Robux. The Terms list what does count (Marketplace and Creator Store listings, Creator Rewards, in-game purchases, ad revenue, affiliate fees) and what does not:

- Robux you bought or redeemed from gift cards
- monthly subscription grants
- trading or reselling items you did not create
- passes sold on template games with no real visits
- money from content that was later moderated

Roblox alone decides the category. If Roblox refunds a player after a complaint about your content, it may claw the same amount back from your Earned Robux.

## Copying and "experience rules"

- You may not copy or modify someone else's item or game and publish it as yours without written permission. Roblox can suspend or terminate accounts for this.
- You may post your own **Experience Rules**, but they must be clearly visible in the game and can never conflict with Roblox rules. Players appeal your in-game bans to you, not to Roblox.
- If a big share of your players egregiously break Roblox rules and you do nothing, Roblox may moderate the game itself.

## Laws you are personally responsible for

The Creator Terms say you must follow all laws that apply to your game, naming loot boxes, dark patterns and potentially addictive features. Roblox tools (PolicyService, odds disclosure) help, but compliance is still on you.

## Disputes between creators

Creator-vs-creator or creator-vs-player disputes require mediation before any lawsuit (IP takedowns excluded). If Roblox steps in, its decision is final, including deducting Robux.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Every asset in the place is yours, licensed, or a Roblox-provided asset used only on Roblox
- [ ] No "Roblox"/"Blox" in the title
- [ ] Revenue plan relies only on Earned Robux sources
- [ ] In-game rules page exists if you ban players for custom rules
