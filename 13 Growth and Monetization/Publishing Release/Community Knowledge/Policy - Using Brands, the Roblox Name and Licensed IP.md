# Using Brands, the Roblox Name and Licensed IP

> Not legal advice. Summary of the Roblox Name and Logo guidelines, the Creator Terms trademark clauses and the Creator Hub "IP licensing for creators" page, checked 2026-10-25.

Real cars, real guns, famous characters, logos on shop signs, and "Roblox" in your title are all common in Roblox games, and all are IP risks. There are three separate questions: can you use **Roblox's** brand, can you use **someone else's** brand, and can you get a **license** to do it properly.

## Roblox's own brand

- **No "Roblox", "Blox" or anything Roblox considers similar** in game titles (games published on or after 2022-06-22), usernames, display names or community names. "Bloxburg"-style names on new games break this.
- **Logos:** the Roblox logo and the "tilt" are not for general use. Only the **"Now on Roblox" badge** may be used in your off-platform promotion, following its usage guide. Non-commercial digital use under the guide needs no approval; offline or commercial use needs written approval via Roblox's request form.
- **No implied partnership:** never suggest Roblox endorses or partners with you.
- **No merch:** the Roblox name, badge, logos and Classic Avatars (R6/R15) may not appear on physical products, packaging, NFTs, film, TV, books or shows without a licensing deal. Roblox says it does not grant unsolicited requests.
- **Coding books** may use the name (not as the first word, cover marked UNOFFICIAL) but never the logo.
- **Press releases** that mention Roblox should be submitted for approval.

## Other people's brands and characters

Roblox's IP page and the Creator Terms say you must own or be authorised for everything you publish. In practice this covers:

- Character likenesses (anime, film, other games), including "inspired by" copies that keep distinctive designs.
- Real brand logos on billboards, cars, clothing or food in your map.
- Real named products (a specific car model, a branded weapon). Treat these as risky; we did not verify any individual maker's policy (unverified).
- Music and sound recordings (see the music note).
- Names in your title or description used to borrow traffic ("Pokemon Simulator").

Rights holders can claim or remove your game through the Rights Manager, and the Creator Terms mention IP tools that let a rights holder **claim content in your game** instead of removing it.

Safe defaults: invent brand names and turn real vehicles into generic ones. Making a fan game free or non-commercial does **not** protect it; Roblox's IP page says unmonetised use can still infringe.

## Getting a license: the Licenses catalog

Roblox runs an IP licensing system (Creator Hub > Explore > **Licenses**).

- **Full game license:** build an entire game on an IP. You submit a request with a pitch for a **public** game, accept the rights holder's revenue share, duration, content standards and brand guidelines. Perpetual licenses can be approved, rejected or conditionally approved; time-limited ones have proposed date ranges (midnight UTC).
- **In-game sales license:** permission to sell IP-based developer products and passes inside a specific eligible game.
- Your licensed content must follow the rights holder's conditions and revenue share for the whole term.

## Pre-launch IP checklist

- [ ] Title and description contain no third-party brand you do not license
- [ ] Map signage, vehicles, weapons and posters use invented names and logos
- [ ] Thumbnail and icon contain no copyrighted characters
- [ ] Off-platform marketing uses only the "Now on Roblox" badge and says "on Roblox", not "official"
- [ ] Any licensed IP has its agreement and brand guide saved in the team drive
