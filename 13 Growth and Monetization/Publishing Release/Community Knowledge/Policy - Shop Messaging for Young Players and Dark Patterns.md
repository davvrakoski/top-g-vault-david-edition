# Shop Messaging for Young Players and Dark Patterns

> Not legal advice. Based on the Help Center "Promoting Items to Younger Audiences" (edited 2026-04-29), the Creator Terms "Restrictions on Use", the Advertising Standards and the Community Standards, checked 2026-10-25.

Roblox's audience is young, and regulators in several regions now look at "dark patterns" (design that pressures or tricks people into spending). The Creator Terms say you must follow all laws on **loot boxes, dark patterns and other potentially addictive features**. Roblox's guidance is short and practical: make promotions suitable for all ages by default, use PolicyService for restricted features, and drop high-pressure language.

## Roblox's own wording guidance

The help article contrasts pushy phrases with calm ones:

| Avoid | Prefer |
| --- | --- |
| "GET IT NOW" | "View Item" |
| "LAST CHANCE, ACT NOW" | "See Price" |
| "BUY BEFORE IT'S GONE!" | "Open Shop" |

## Rules from the Advertising Standards worth copying into your shop

These rules apply to ads aimed at under-18s, but they are a good bar for any in-game promotion:

- Do not directly urge children to buy, or to ask a parent to buy.
- Do not imply the item gives status, popularity or skill it does not.
- Do not describe prices with "only" or "just".
- Do not mimic system UI (fake notifications, fake close buttons, fake Roblox prompts).

## Hard rules (not just style)

- **Odds disclosure** for paid random items, with PolicyService gating.
- **Rewarded video:** opt-in click, predetermined reward, no forced videos.
- **No soliciting donations** for causes; a pure tip jar is allowed only if the player gets nothing back.
- **No fake "free Robux"** or misleading offers anywhere.
- **No misleading metadata** (titles, thumbnails, descriptions that promise what the game does not have).

## Patterns to avoid (our checklist, not official wording)

- Countdown timers that reset for each player to fake scarcity.
- Purchase prompts that open automatically on spawn or after a death.
- "Confirm" buttons styled to look like "Cancel", or a hidden close button.
- Prices shown only in a premium currency with odd bundle sizes so players cannot tell the Robux cost.
- Social shaming ("everyone else has this").
- Pity systems or luck items whose real effect is not shown numerically.
- Re-prompting after a player declines, especially if a parental spending limit blocked them.

## Copy patterns that work

- Neutral calls to action: "View", "Details", "Open Shop".
- Show the exact Robux price on the button the player presses.
- Explain what the item does in one plain sentence.
- Put limited-time items on a real, global end date displayed in the player's time zone.
- Let players close every offer with one obvious tap.

## Why it pays off

Calm shops convert fine and protect your Kids/Select eligibility, your maturity rating, and your standing if a regulator or parent complains. See [[Ethical Monetization Lines - Where Devs Draw Pay-to-Win]] for community views on where the lines are.
