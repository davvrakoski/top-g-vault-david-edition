# Moderation Appeals for Games, Assets and Accounts

> Not legal advice. Summary of the Help Center "Appeal Your Content or Account Moderation" and "Content Moderation on Roblox", plus the appeal steps in the content maturity doc, checked 2026-10-25.

Sooner or later an asset, a thumbnail or a whole game gets moderated, sometimes by mistake. Appeals have strict limits (one review, 30 days, owner only), so a rushed or vague appeal can waste the only chance. Treat a moderation hit like a production incident: freeze, gather facts, then appeal once with a clear case.

## How moderation happens

- **Uploads** (images, meshes, audio, video) pass a multi-step review before going live. Audio is scanned for IP infringement.
- **Published or updated games** are scanned by tools for problem language, filter bypasses and policy issues; flagged games go to human reviewers.
- **User reports** and trusted flaggers feed the queue.
- Consequences scale from warning, to content removal, to account restrictions, considering your history; repeat violations escalate.

## The two appeal routes

1. **Violations & Appeals** page (roblox.com/report-appeals): your moderation notice links here when the category is eligible. Choose the violation, click **Request Appeal**, explain, **Send**, and track status there. Accepted appeals show "Appeal accepted".
2. **Support form** (roblox.com/support) when the violation is not listed. For content: username, short reason, **Asset ID**, link to the content. For a game's maturity moderation: category **Appeal a Decision**, subcategory "I was wrongly moderated for other content I created", game ID in the Asset ID field.

For group-owned games, the **group owner** appeals; for user games, the owner.

## Hard limits

- Appeal within **30 days** of the action (EU users: **6 months**).
- Each moderation action is reviewed **once**; duplicates are ignored.
- Only the account owner may appeal, from the account's valid email.
- Most moderated content cannot be restored even if the appeal succeeds; keep source files.

EU users can also go to a certified out-of-court dispute settlement body or court under the DSA. UK users have OSA reporting and appeal rights.

## Writing an appeal that works

- Name the exact asset or game ID and what the notice said.
- Explain **why** it complies, referring to the rule (for example "the blood is green and disappears in 2 seconds, which is unrealistic blood under the questionnaire").
- If it was a genuine mistake, say what you changed. Fixing first and then appealing is often the faster path.
- Stay short and factual. No threats, no "my friend's game has worse".

## Team incident checklist

- [ ] Stop publishing until you know what was flagged
- [ ] Screenshot the notice and record the date (30-day clock)
- [ ] Identify the asset, text or feature; search the place for similar items
- [ ] Decide: appeal, or fix and re-upload a compliant version
- [ ] One person submits the appeal from the owner account
- [ ] Log outcome in the release notes / compliance log

## What not to do

- Do not re-upload the same flagged asset under a new ID; that looks like evasion.
- Do not create an alt account to keep publishing; ban evasion is a Community Standards violation.
