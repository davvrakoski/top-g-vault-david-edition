*Explains ways to continue learning about creating animations in Studio.*

Congratulations on completing your first animation with Roblox Studio! Now that you have experience working with keyframes and posing characters, you can share your work with others and extend your skills by learning about Studio's additional animation features.

## Share with others

When you first publish a game, it's only playable by you as the creator. However, creating on Roblox is always more fun when you can share what you make with others. For steps on inviting early testers to preview your game, see [[30 Official Roblox Docs/Production/Publishing/Create and publish games and places#Invite early testers|Invite early testers]]. For steps on how to release your game to players, see [[30 Official Roblox Docs/Production/Publishing/Create and publish games and places#Release games|Release games]].

## Continue learning

The basic **Animation Editor** tools are an excellent start while learning to create animations, but Studio offers many additional animation features to add liveliness to your characters and environment, including:

- [[30 Official Roblox Docs/Animation/Animation events|Event markers]] that trigger custom behavior at specific keyframes in an animation.
- [[30 Official Roblox Docs/Animation/Inverse Kinematics|Inverse Kinematics]] that let you pose and animate multiple character parts by posing or adjusting a single object.

- The [[30 Official Roblox Docs/Animation/Curve Editor|Curve Editor]] interface that lets you see and modify how a rig's position and orientation changes between keyframes through color-coded curve graphs.
