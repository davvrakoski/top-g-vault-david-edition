# Level Design Reading List for Roblox Builders

Free reading that actually changes how you build maps, checked 2026-10-25. Each entry says what it's good for and what you may do with it. "Link only" means read it and link it; don't copy its text or images into your own products.

## General level design
| Resource | Author | Good for | License / use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Level Design Book** (book.leveldesignbook.com) | Community book, no single author named on the home page | The best free overview: process (layout, blockout, lighting, playtesting), typologies, pacing, balance, door scripting | CC BY-NC-SA 4.0: non-commercial; link only for paid products |
| The Level Design Book: Recommended talks | same | A curated list of GDC and other talks | Link only |
| **Level Layout, Disneyland Style** (above-the-garage.com/wienies) | Stephen Clarke-Willson | "Weenies", hub and spoke, forced perspective, hiding edges | Copyright 2000, no open license; link only |
| **The Image of the City** (book, 1960) | Kevin Lynch | Paths, edges, districts, nodes, landmarks | Book; buy or borrow |

Vault notes that apply these: [[Wayfinding with Landmarks and Weenies]], [[Sightlines, Cover and Chokepoints for PvP Maps]], [[Horror Map Design - Pacing, Loops and Readable Darkness]].

## How to use this list
1. Read The Level Design Book's process overview once, start to finish.
2. Do Roblox's environmental art curriculum with its sample place; it's the closest thing to an official Roblox level-design course.
3. Before each new map, re-read the chapter for its genre (balance for PvP, pacing for horror, Ruski #5 for open worlds).
4. Before release, read MrChickenRocket's article and run [[Map Audit Command Bar Script - Parts, Mesh IDs, Collision and Shadows]].
