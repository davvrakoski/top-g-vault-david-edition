# Narrative for Short Sessions - Cold Opens, Beats and Recaps

Roblox players browse a sort of game shelf, join, decide within moments whether to stay, and often play in short bursts between other games, on a phone, with friends talking over voice chat. A story built like a novel (slow first act, long exposition, payoff in hour three) is never seen. Mobile narrative designers face the same conditions. A 2015 Game Developer article on Zynga narrative designer Christy Marx describes game writing as "the haiku of storytelling", and Valentina Tamer (author of a book on narrative design for mobile and live games) notes in an articy interview that mobile players often cannot give the story their full attention. This note turns those constraints into a structure for Roblox story games.

## The session is the unit

Design the story in **session-sized arcs** of about 10-25 minutes, each complete on its own:

1. **Cold open (first 60 seconds).** A strong image or event before any menu or text: the elevator doors open on a ruined hotel; your friend is dragged away. Players should know *what to do* (escape, find, rescue) without reading.
2. **First objective in under 2 minutes.** Concrete and visible ("Find the key in the kitchen").
3. **Beats every 2-4 minutes.** Something changes: a reveal, a new area, a threat, a joke. Silence between beats is fine; nothing-happening is not.
4. **Midpoint twist** around halfway.
5. **Climax and ending** with a satisfying close, plus **one hook** for next time.

This matches the chapter structure in [[Story - Chapter-Based Games - Places, Teleports and Chapter Select]].

## Tell it in layers

| Layer | For whom | Form |
|---|---|---|
| **Surface** | Everyone, including players who skip everything | Goals, visuals, sounds, the monster |
| **Characters** | Most players | Short lines, barks, faces, a companion |
| **Lore** | Fans | Notes, tapes, hidden rooms ([[Story - Lore Drip - Notes, Tapes and Collectibles That Players Read]]) |

The surface must make sense alone. A player who skips every text box should still finish the chapter knowing roughly what happened.

## Rules from mobile narrative that fit Roblox

From the Christy Marx article:

- **Write concisely**; small word counts force better lines.
- **Repeat key information**, phrased differently each time, because players take story in small pieces.
- **Use modular, episodic structure** instead of one final goal; avoid ending the whole game when the main villain falls. Give arcs that players can pick up and leave, and add new arcs over time.
- **Breadcrumb** small story pieces across separate quests so they build into a bigger story.
- **Avoid pacing bottlenecks**, such as quests that all wait on one locked resource.

## Recaps and re-entry

Players return after days. Every session start should answer "where am I and what was I doing?":

- A **"Previously..." card** (one image, one sentence) on chapter start or rejoin.
- The **current objective** pinned on screen for the first minute.
- NPCs who **mention the last event** once ("You made it out of the basement!").

## Group play and voice chat

- Assume players are **talking over** your story. Put key beats in visuals and short captions, not long voiced monologues.
- **Do not split the group** for story moments unless you show each player something; split-screen-style personal scenes leave others waiting.
- Group cutscenes stay short and skippable by vote ([[Story - Multiplayer Cutscene Sync - Server Clock, Late Joiners and Skip Votes]]).

## Roblox's own starter

Roblox's education course *Build It, Play It: Story Games* walks beginners through writing and coding a simple story game. It is a good first exercise for a team member who has never written interactive text.

## Checklist

- [ ] The goal is clear in 60 s without reading.
- [ ] A beat happens at least every 4 minutes (time a playtest).
- [ ] The chapter makes sense with all text skipped.
- [ ] Session ends on a hook and a reward.
- [ ] Re-entry recap exists.
