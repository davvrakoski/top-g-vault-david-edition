# Case Study - Grow a Garden

Grow a Garden is an idle farming game that went from about 500 concurrent players to the biggest concurrent audience any game had ever recorded within months of its March 2025 launch. It matters because the core idea is simple enough for anyone to copy, so the lessons are in the details: offline growth, timed shop restocks, visible gardens and weekly live updates.

## Snapshot (Roblox page, seen 2026-10-25)
| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Visits | 36.0 billion |
| Playing | ~19.9k (far below peak) |
| Favorites | 11.5 million |
| Likes | ~90% of 10.5M votes |
| Server size | 4 (Roblox field; actual garden plots per server may differ) |
| Created / updated | 2025-03-25 / 2026-10-24 |
| Game passes for sale | 0 |

Peaks: over 16 million on 2025-06-21 and at least 22.3 million on 2025-08-23 (Wikipedia list). GamesBeat reports ~60 million DAU at peak. Won Best New Experience and People's Choice at the 2025 Innovation Awards (press). A film adaptation was announced 2025-11-05.

## Core loop
1. Buy seeds from a shop that restocks on a timer.
2. Plant them on your plot; wait for growth.
3. Harvest and sell; buy rarer seeds and gear.
4. Hope for mutations (weather and events add value multipliers to crops).
The page says it plainly: your garden grows while you are offline.

## Session shape
Very short check-ins (plant, sell, catch the restock) plus long AFK sessions during events. The restock timer creates many small reasons to return per day.

## Progression and economy
- Seed rarity ladder; mutations multiply crop value (community guides list multipliers from x5 to x150, unverified).
- Server-wide weather changes randomly; some events are only triggered by the developers live ("admin" events, per fan guides).
- Pets and gear add passive bonuses (unverified detail).

## Monetization (store page, 2026-10-25)
No game passes. Monetization runs through in-game Developer Products (buying seeds or restocks with Robux, gifting), per community guides (unverified). GamesBeat's interview does not discuss monetization.

## Social hooks
- Gardens are visible to everyone in the server, so rare plants are a flex.
- Stealing and trading features were added over time (unverified).
- Live developer-triggered events made people join the same moment, like a broadcast.

## Update cadence
Splitting Point (Janzen "Jandel" Madsen's studio, which partnered with the original 16-year-old creator) runs weekly updates across its games (GamesBeat).

## Why it grew
- Universal theme ("as generic as you can get", per Madsen) plus offline growth, which he calls new for Roblox.
- A partner studio took a small hit and scaled live-ops: ~500 CCU at partnership, ~100k two weeks later, ~1M about a month later (GamesBeat).
- The platform's 2025 surge (record platform CCU of 47.3M on 2025-08-23) lifted it further.

## Lessons you can copy
1. **Let progress happen while away.** Offline growth turns every return into a reward moment.
2. **Use a global restock clock.** Timed stock gives a reason to check in and makes rare stock feel like an event.
3. **Make wealth visible.** Plots in a shared server do the marketing for rare items.
4. **Ship weekly.** A predictable weekly drop builds a habit and a content calendar for creators.
5. **Partner instead of grinding alone.** A small game with good retention can be scaled by a team that knows live-ops.
