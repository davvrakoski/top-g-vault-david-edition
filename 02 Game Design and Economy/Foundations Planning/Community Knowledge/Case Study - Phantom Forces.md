# Case Study - Phantom Forces

Phantom Forces (StyLiS Studios) is a military FPS with deep loadouts: four weapon classes, attachments, maps and modes, compared by critics to Call of Duty, Battlefield and Counter-Strike. It matters because it is the long-running proof that a "serious" shooter can live on Roblox when the gunplay and unlock depth are good, and it monetizes through cases and skins rather than passes.

Three Bloxy Awards; about 10k daily players in 2018 (Wikipedia list).

## Core loop
1. Choose a class and loadout (primary, secondary, melee, grenade; attachments).
2. Play a team mode on a map; movement includes crouch, prone, slide and lean-style cover use (Wikipedia mentions crouch, prone, flank, jump to cover).
3. Earn XP and credits; level up to unlock guns; kill with a weapon to unlock its attachments.
4. Open cases for skins; customize.

## Session shape
Matches of several minutes with continuous rotation; long sessions are common because unlock goals are always near.

## Progression and economy
- Rank unlocks weapons; per-weapon kills unlock attachments ("thousands of possible weapon loadouts", page).
- Credits can buy weapons early (community wiki detail, unverified).
- Cases and skins form the cosmetic economy; the current update adds a melee as a case unlock and the ability to rotate skins (page).

## Monetization (store page, 2026-10-25)
No game passes. Revenue comes from in-game Robux purchases of credits and cases (Developer Products; community wiki, unverified detail). A shooter with zero passes avoids "paid gun" perception.

## Social hooks
- Squads and team modes; competitive identity around guns and stats.
- Patch notes in the description list every new and remodeled weapon, which the community debates.

## Update cadence
Seasonal big patches (the page shows a Fall 2026 update with new DMR, AR, carbine, PDW, melee and many remodels) and limited event modes ("[Infection!]" in the title).

## Why it grew
- Gunplay quality and complexity well above what players expected from Roblox in 2015 (Wikipedia notes praise for design, controls and complexity).
- Long unlock trees keep players for years.
- Fair: you cannot buy power directly through passes.

## Lessons you can copy
1. **Per-weapon unlock trees.** Unlocking attachments by using a gun gives every weapon its own mini progression.
2. **Detailed patch notes are marketing.** A shooter community reads weapon lists closely.
3. **Remodel old content.** Updating classic guns refreshes the game without new systems.
4. **Event modes in the title.** A bracket tag like "[Infection!]" signals a limited mode.
5. **No passes can be a feature.** For competitive shooters, keep paid items cosmetic and say so.
