# Case Study - Adopt Me!

Adopt Me! (Uplift Games) turned a family roleplay hangout into the longest-running pet collection and trading economy on Roblox. It matters because it shows how a soft, low-skill game can hold players for years when the real product is the collection and the trade between players, not the activity loop.

Historic: averaged ~600k concurrent players in June 2020 and passed 10 billion plays by July 2020 (Wikipedia list).

## Core loop
1. Get a pet (eggs bought with in-game Bucks or Robux, event rewards, trading).
2. Care for it: needs pop up (hungry, sleepy, bored), you fulfil them at locations around the town, which pays Bucks and ages the pet.
3. Grow it up through age stages; combine four fully grown copies into a Neon, four Neons into a Mega Neon (community wiki rule).
4. Trade with other players to complete the collection or chase rarer pets.
5. Spend on your house and furniture; show off.

## Session shape
Short, repeatable care tasks (each a minute or two), so a session can be 10 minutes or two hours. Trading sessions are open-ended and social.

## Progression and economy
- Progression is horizontal: rarity tiers, Neon/Mega Neon upgrades and limited event pets, not levels or power.
- Scarcity is created by time: event pets leave the shop, so old pets gain trade value.
- The Neon rule is a built-in sink: four pets become one, which removes supply.

## Monetization (store page, 2026-10-25)
Six game passes for sale: 1.25x Candy Multiplier (50, event-limited), Premium Plots (285), +1 Pet Pen Slot (295), VIP (480: room, limo, house, chat tag), Cozy Home Lure (765), Millionaire Pack (795). Most revenue comes from in-game Robux purchases of eggs, pets and currency (Developer Products). Wikipedia cites Uplift revenue above US$16 million and notes the game was the top earner on the platform in the UK in 2022.

## Social hooks
- Trading is the main social glue: you need other people to finish a collection.
- Houses are visitable; roleplay as parent/baby gives a reason to group up.
- The game page asks players to press Notify so they learn about updates.

## Update cadence
Seasonal events (Halloween "Where Bear?" on the page now) plus regular new pet drops. A licensing interview with Uplift staff says hero pets for products are chosen from the most traded or most wanted pets, using an internal data analyst.

## Why it grew
- The nominal theme is family adoption roleplay, but the de facto focus became pets and trading (Wikipedia list), which put a collection goal on top of a hangout.
- Very young audience, simple controls, bright readable silhouettes.
- Trading economy made rare pets valuable outside the game (some sold for up to US$100, which also drew scammers).

## Lessons you can copy
1. **Give a hangout a collection layer.** Roleplay alone churns; something to collect and show off makes people return.
2. **Use merge-to-upgrade as a sink.** "N copies become one better copy" removes supply and keeps trade values meaningful.
3. **Make scarcity time-based, not drop-rate based.** Retiring event items is easier to understand for kids than 0.01% odds.
4. **Plan anti-scam UX from day one.** A trade window that shows both sides clearly, plus confirmation steps, protects a young audience.
5. **Design pets as silhouettes.** Uplift's art rule (strong shapes, bold colors) makes items readable at thumbnail size and on merch.
