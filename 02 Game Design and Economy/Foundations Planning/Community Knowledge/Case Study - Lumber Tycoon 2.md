# Case Study - Lumber Tycoon 2

Lumber Tycoon 2 (Defaultio, solo developer) is a physics sandbox: chop trees with an axe, haul logs by truck, process them at a sawmill, sell planks, and spend money on better axes, vehicles and building parts for your base. It matters because it shows that a slow, physical, sometimes frustrating loop can create a legendary game when the physics itself is the fun, and its passes sell around world tolls.

## Core loop
1. Buy an axe; walk into the forest; chop a tree (physics cut).
2. Load logs onto a truck by hand; drive to your base.
3. Saw logs into planks with a sawmill; sell or build with them.
4. Buy better axes (cut faster, cut rare wood), bigger trucks, sawmills, and blueprint parts.
5. Travel to remote biomes for rare wood types (often behind toll bridges and ferries).

## Session shape
Long sessions: a single rare-wood trip can take a long time. Losing a load (logs falling off a truck) is a classic moment.

## Progression and economy
- Axes and wood types form the ladder; rare woods are worth far more.
- Base building with blueprints is the main sink.
- Trading of rare axes and items between players (community knowledge; unverified for today's economy).

## Monetization (store page, 2026-10-25)
Three passes, each a world-friction skip:
- Super Bridge (220): lower the toll bridge for free, for longer.
- Super Hoove (390): skip the ferry ticket.
- Super Blueprints (720): fill any blueprint instantly instead of placing planks by hand.
The joke-style descriptions ("maybe Hoove won't notice") match the game's tone.

## Social hooks
- Bases are visible; friends help haul and build.
- Shared physics chaos (players stealing or knocking logs) creates stories.
- Changelog in the game menu (page) keeps the community informed.

## Update cadence
Infrequent; updated on the day of this snapshot. The game lives on long-term goals more than content drops.

## Why it grew
- The physical loop (cut, carry, drive, saw) is satisfying and unusual.
- Rare biomes and items gave a deep exploration and collection goal.
- Mysteries and secrets in the world fed community videos (unverified as a measured cause).

## Lessons you can copy
1. **Make the work physical.** Carrying real objects is more memorable than a progress bar.
2. **Put tolls in the world, then sell skips.** A bridge or ferry fee is a soft sink; a pass to skip it is fair.
3. **Sell automation of the boring part.** Instant blueprint fill keeps creative control while removing busywork.
4. **Hide rare resources far away.** Distance plus risk is what makes rare wood valuable.
5. **Small servers for physics games.** Six players keeps physics affordable on the server.
