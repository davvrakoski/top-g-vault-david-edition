# Case Study - Piggy

Piggy (MiniToon, Kohl Couture) is an episodic horror escape game: players solve item puzzles to escape a map while one player or a bot plays Piggy, the hunter. It matters because it proved that weekly story chapters on Roblox can build a huge fandom fast, and that a creator's own channel can be the main growth engine.

Uploaded January 2020; story finale 2020-05-25; Book 2 from 2020-09-12 to 2021-10-23; main story declared over 2022-11-24; Piggy: Intercity full release 2026-06-13 (Wikipedia list). It held the record of 1 billion visits in 83 days until Blade Ball (Pocket Tactics).

## Core loop
1. Pick a chapter map; one player is Piggy (or a bot), others are survivors.
2. Find items (keys, tools) and use them on doors and locks in the right order.
3. Avoid or trap Piggy; one hit and you are out.
4. Escape to win; earn Piggy Tokens; buy skins and traps.

## Session shape
A chapter run is short (minutes). Small servers (6) keep it tense and personal.

## Progression and economy
- Chapters as content (story progression is the main "unlock").
- Tokens buy skins; skins of story characters are the collectible.
- Build Mode lets players make their own maps (credited on the page to Optikk and Epic_Tank).

## Monetization (store page, 2026-10-25)
Eight passes: six Build Map Slots (100 each), x2 Piggy Chance (150, more often chosen as the hunter), x2 Piggy Tokens (300). Skins sold in-game. The "chance to be the monster" pass sells a role, not power.

## Social hooks
- Asymmetric play: being the hunter is the fun role and is what people want.
- Story lore and cliffhangers drove fan theories and videos.
- Build Mode and its sharing make players into level designers.

## Update cadence
In 2020, new chapters arrived about weekly with cliffhangers (community wiki history, unverified exact rhythm). Today: seasonal events ("Frightful Carnival" in the title).

## Why it grew
- Combined familiar elements (a Granny-style hunt and a cartoon pig character, per Wikipedia) into a Roblox-native episodic story.
- MiniToon's YouTube streams fed the fandom; one June 2020 stream reportedly brought ~30k subscribers in a day (fan wiki).
- Episodes gave creators a fresh video every week.

## Lessons you can copy
1. **Release story in episodes.** Each chapter is a new trailer, a new video for creators, and a reason to return.
2. **Sell the fun role's odds.** "x2 chance to be the hunter" is valued and fair.
3. **Let fans build levels.** A build mode with paid slots extends content cheaply.
4. **Teach with item-lock puzzles.** Key -> door chains are readable for young players and easy to vary.
5. **Own a channel.** A dev who streams their own game controls the hype cycle.
