# Case Study - Da Hood

Da Hood (Da Hood Entertainment) is a hardcore open-world street PvP game: cops vs criminals, banks and shops to rob, guns you can lose, and a skill-based melee and gun combat meta. It matters as a counter-example in this set: it is big and long-lived but has the lowest like ratio here (~71%), showing the trade-off of a punishing, toxic-prone PvP sandbox.

## Core loop
From the page's own tips:
1. Pick a side: cops (go to the station; executing anyone without cuffs gets you kicked off the team) or criminals (ransack banks and shops for cash and wanted level).
2. Buy guns and gear; train muscle at the gym (eat lettuce to reduce it).
3. Fight: blocking and timing ("weaving") matter.
4. Gun saves can be lost on death, combat logging, or empty ammo; a cash drop takes 30%.

## Session shape
Open-ended PvP sessions. Loss is real (guns and cash), so sessions swing between careful and chaotic.

## Progression and economy
- Cash from crime or jobs buys guns, food, armor and cosmetics.
- Loss on death (gear, part of cash) is the main sink and the reason fights matter.
- Crews and reputation are social progression (unverified).

## Monetization (store page, 2026-10-25)
14 passes, mostly small (15-199 Robux):
- Tools/weapons: Flashlight (15), Knife (65), Bat (110), Pepper Spray (150), Mask to hide display name (175).
- Ownership/QoL: Vehicle Lock (89), House Limit Boost (100).
- Expression: Custom Cursor (50), Animation Packs (100 each), Custom Ringtone (100), Anonymous Calls (100), Boombox (120), Hair Glue (199, stops hair being snatched).
Many passes are about self-expression and protecting your look, a real concern in a street PvP game.

## Social hooks
- Crews, rivalries and reputation; large servers (45).
- Phone features (calls, ringtones) support roleplay.
- Content creators film fights and "trolling" videos (unverified as a measured cause).

## Update cadence
Ongoing; updated the day before this snapshot.

## Why it grew
- A gritty, high-stakes alternative to family-friendly town games.
- Skill-based combat with loss gives strong emotions and video moments.
- The account-age gate is an anti-alt and anti-exploit measure.

## Lessons you can copy
1. **Loss makes fights matter, and makes players angry.** If you add item loss, expect lower ratings; balance with protection options.
2. **Gate by account age to fight alts.** A simple 10-day rule cuts throwaway exploit accounts.
3. **Write rules into team roles.** Cops who abuse force lose the role automatically; systemic rules beat moderation.
4. **Sell protection of identity and style.** Hair Glue and Mask passes show what players value in a griefing-heavy space.
5. **Put tips on the page.** Da Hood uses its description as a manual for a hard game.
