# Case Study - 99 Nights in the Forest

99 Nights in the Forest (Grandma's Favourite Games: Alec Kieft, Cameron Angland, Matthew Hufton, New Zealand) is a co-op survival horror game: keep a campfire burning, craft defenses, explore a forest for resources and rescue four lost kids while the Deer and cultists hunt you. It matters most for survival and zombie projects: it shows how a single central object (the fire) and a countdown goal ("99 nights") make a clear, social survival loop.

## Core loop
1. Spawn at camp with your squad; pick a class (community wiki detail, unverified).
2. Day: go out to chop wood, gather scrap and food; kill wolves or bears to earn keys to caves.
3. Feed the campfire to keep it lit and grow the safe zone; craft defenses and tools.
4. Night: defend the fire against the Deer and cultists.
5. Rescue the four missing kids from caves; survive as many nights as possible toward 99.

## Session shape
A run is long and resumable only by surviving; nights give a rhythm of calm gathering and tense defense. Death of the group ends the run.

## Progression and economy
- In-run: fire level, crafting bench upgrades and loot.
- Meta: Diamonds and classes unlocked between runs (community wiki detail, unverified).
- The "99" counter is a visible score that players brag about.

## Monetization (store page, 2026-10-25)
Only one pass: Decorator Pass (199, hammer and paintbrush to decorate your base). Other monetization is in-game (Developer Products such as premium currency for classes, per community guides; unverified).

## Social hooks
- The fire is a shared responsibility: someone must feed it while others explore.
- Rescuing the kids gives a co-op mission with clear completion.
- Horror stalker (the Deer) creates scream moments for streams.

## Update cadence
Frequent; updated on the day of this snapshot.

## Why it grew
- "Survive N nights" is an instantly understood goal and a shareable score.
- Team members had shipped other Roblox hits (Mall Tycoon, Wacky Wizards, Break In) per TheWrap, so they knew the platform.
- Arrived during the 2025 platform surge and was boosted by awards and media deals.

## Lessons you can copy
1. **One central object to protect.** A campfire, generator or train gives the group a shared home and a fail state.
2. **Count the score in days or nights.** "Night 47" is easier to brag about than points.
3. **Day/night as pacing.** Gather in safety, defend in danger; it creates natural tension waves.
4. **Add a rescue goal.** Finding lost NPCs gives exploration a purpose beyond loot.
5. **Keep the pass store tiny if the core is strong.** One decorating pass plus in-game currency was enough.
